By request from Betsy Weber, I’m revealing my granny’s shortbread recipe (with permission).

<strong>Ingredients</strong>
<ul>
	<li>200g plain flour</li>
	<li>100g Butter</li>
	<li>50g Castor Sugar (called superfine sugar in the US)</li>
</ul>
<strong>Method</strong>
<ul>
	<li>Mix the flour and sugar together</li>
	<li>Cut the butter in to pieces.</li>
	<li>Using fingertips rub it into the mixture until evenly distributed (almost like bread crumbs)</li>
	<li>Knead the mixture together into a soft but <span style="text-decoration:underline;">not</span> sticky dough.</li>
	<li>Divide the mixture and shape into rounds (about 1cm thick).</li>
	<li>Place rounds on greased baking tray(s)</li>
	<li>Bake at gas mark 3 / 325°F / 160°C for about 45 minutes until the shortbread is a pale golden colour.</li>
	<li>Cool before storing in an air-tight container</li>
</ul>
<strong>Variations</strong>

Instead of rounds, the mix can be spread out over a rectangular tray and cut in to fingers after baking, but before cooling.

I should also note that following the recipe above will not give the same results as the 8 decades worth of expertise in lovingly baking shortbread that my granny has. As a child the first thing I asked for when visiting was always: Did you make any shortbread?
