At work we're upgrading to Outlook 2013. One of the new features is that the Calendar will display the weather for the next few days.

<img class="aligncenter" alt="Outlook 2013 Displaying the Weather in Fahrenheit" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-Weather-in-Fahrenheit.png" width="524" height="61" />

If you like this, that's great. However, most of the world uses Celcius, but out of the box Outlook displays Fahrenheit (regardless of the locale set up on your machine). In fact, it also defaults to New York as the city.
<h3>Changing the Weather Location</h3>
It is easy enough to change the weather location. Just click on the name of the city and you get a drop down box.

<img class="aligncenter" alt="Weather Location Drop Down Box" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-Weather-Location-DropDown.png" width="155" height="123" />

If the city you want is not in the list, just select "Add Location" and you get a search box to type in the city that you want.

<img class="aligncenter" alt="Search for Weather Location" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-Change-Weather-Location.png" width="239" height="198" />
<h3>Change the Temperature Scale to Celsius</h3>
Changing the temperature scale to Celsius is a little bit more involved. First click the "File" menu in the top right corner of the window.

<img class="aligncenter" alt="File Menu Button in Outlook 2013" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-File-Menu-Button.png" width="171" height="158" />

Then select the "Options" button from the new menu.

<img class="aligncenter" alt="File Menu Details" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-File-Menu-Contents.png" width="316" height="474" />

This will display the options dialog. First, click the "Calendar" button in the left menu. Then scroll down to the bottom of the options that are presented. The Weather options are last. You'll see you can then select the temperature scale ("Celsius" or "Fahrenheit") that you want. Or you can even turn off the weather too.
<p style="text-align:center;"><img class="aligncenter" alt="Options Dialog" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-Options-Dialog.png" width="588" height="479" /></p>
Then you can just "OK" the dialog and the weather will be updated into Celsius. Much more civilised.

<img class="aligncenter" alt="Much more civilised, the temperature in Celsius" src="http://static.colinmackay.co.uk/images/outlook2013/2013-08-01-Final-Result.png" width="516" height="61" />
